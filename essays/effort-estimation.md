## Why Did We Even Have to Do This?
<img src="/img/software_estimates_meme.jpeg" style="float: right; width: 250px; margin: 0 0 10px 15px;" />

When I was first introduced to effort estimation, it felt pretty useless. However, after applying it throughout the final project, I began to see its value. It helped me better understand how I was actually spending my time and gave me a way to reflect on the difference between my initial estimates and the actual time required to complete tasks. My effort estimation process was mostly based on intuition and prior experience from similar assignments. If a task felt familiar, I assumed it would take about the same amount of time, which often led to inconsistent estimates.

## That Was Not 60 Minutes

My estimates were often inaccurate, and I usually underestimated how long tasks would take because I mostly focused on the coding portion and didn’t account for time spent on research, debugging, or using AI tools. Even though my estimates were off, they still helped me figure out what I should be working on and how to pace myself. To track my actual effort, I just noted when I started and finished working on something and then roughly estimated how that time was spent. I didn’t use a timer or any tracking tool, so it was only somewhat accurate. For example, I thought adding a React Bits component would take about 60 minutes, but it ended up taking around 75 minutes, with about 45 minutes spent coding and 30 minutes spent on research and asking ChatGPT for help. This made me realize that I consistently underestimated my time because I wasn’t considering all the non-coding work that goes into completing a task.

## Things I’d Actually Do Next Time

If I were to improve my process, I would focus on making both estimation and tracking more precise. A lot of my estimates were made after I had already completed the work, which meant I had to think back on tasks I did days earlier and try to remember how long they took. Because of that, my estimates and tracking weren’t very accurate. Next time, I would try to estimate tasks before starting them and track my time more consistently while I’m actually working. I would also break tasks into smaller pieces and include extra time for research and debugging. Using a tool like WakaTime or another tracker would also help me get more accurate data and improve my future estimates.

## On a Time Crunch

During the project, I used AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude to help me understand things, fix bugs, and get unstuck when something wasn’t working. I’d usually ask questions about why something wasn’t rendering correctly or how to implement certain UI features, and I also used it to figure out class names for styling like alignment or colors. When it gave me code that matched what I needed, I would sometimes just use those snippets directly, and other times I had to tweak them to fit my issues. It definitely made things easier and helped me move faster, but it also took time to write prompts, go through responses, and test things out before they actually worked.
