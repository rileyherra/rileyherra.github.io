# I. Introduction
<img src="../img/ai-software-engineering.png" style="float: right; width: 250px; margin: 0 0 10px 15px;" />

Artificial Intelligence has become a major part of modern education, especially in software engineering and computer science. Throughout ICS 314, I mainly used ChatGPT and Claude whenever I got stuck on assignments, needed help understanding concepts, or wanted help organizing my thoughts. I used ChatGPT the most because I had access to a business version through my internship, although I generally preferred Claude’s responses whenever I had enough tokens available. Overall, AI became something I relied on regularly both for learning concepts and getting through difficult assignments.

# II. Personal Experience with AI

**Experience WODs:** I used AI for most of the Experience WODs whenever I got stuck or needed help troubleshooting problems. Usually, I would watch the screencasts first and attempt the assignment myself before asking AI for help where I became stuck. I often asked ChatGPT for debugging help, TypeScript examples, or alternative terminal commands whenever the provided instructions or commands were not working properly. However, there was one Experience WOD where AI repeatedly generated solutions far outside the intended scope of the class, which actually caused me to spend almost double the DNF time trying to fix unnecessary complexity. That experience taught me that AI can sometimes make problems worse if the prompts or generated solutions are not carefully reviewed.

**In-class Practice WODs:** I used AI during practice WODs mainly for debugging and frontend issues. For example, I would ask questions about frontend layout issues or why a React component was not rendering correctly. AI helped me become more efficient, although sometimes it gave more complicated solutions than necessary.

**In-class WODs:** I used AI for every in-class WOD except the very first one. I mainly relied on AI during in-class WODs whenever time became a major factor. Whenever AI generated code for me during in-class WODs, I would sometimes ask it to include comments explaining what different sections were doing or generate simple test cases so I could better understand the solution afterward. One time I arrived to class around 30 minutes late, so I had to rely heavily on AI to quickly catch up and troubleshoot issues while still trying to understand what was happening. I ended up finishing the WOD at the very last minute, and that experience showed me how useful AI could be under pressure. However, I still had to carefully review the generated solutions because some outputs were overly complicated or outside the intended scope of the course.

**Essays:** For essays, I usually typed all my ideas and details in an unorganized way and then asked AI to reorganize everything into proper paragraphs. Afterward, I would review the output, fix inaccuracies, adjust grammar, and add my own personal examples. AI was very useful for organizing thoughts and improving the overall flow of my writing.

**Final project:** For the final project, my team created a recycling bin finder application for UH Mānoa that included sorting guides and recycling statistics tracking. It was also my first time seriously working with GitHub in a shared repository with multiple people contributing at once, so AI helped me understand merge conflicts, commit messages, and how to safely push changes without breaking the main branch. I mainly worked on the visual/frontend side, and AI helped me integrate TypeScript/React components into our existing codebase while using our `global.css` styling setup without interfering with the rest of the pages. One specific thing I remember using AI for was helping me create hover effects that changed the colors of cards and fonts whenever users moved their cursor over them. I also used AI to help explain issues created by other team members’ code and suggest possible approaches whenever I assigned myself a GitHub issue to work on. Since it was my first experience working in a collaborative repository, AI also made contributing to the project feel less intimidating whenever I was unsure about modifying shared code.

**Learning a concept / tutorial:** AI helped me better understand frontend concepts related to positioning, spacing, and responsive layouts. One concept I struggled with was positioning and spacing because margins and padding would sometimes behave differently than I expected depending on the container or layout I was using. AI explanations and examples made it easier to understand how different layout properties interacted with each other. I also sometimes used AI to generate practice problems similar to the WODs or create more interactive explanations while working through coding camp tutorials.

**Answering a question in class or in Discord:** I did not really use AI for answering questions in class or Discord. Usually, I preferred searching through existing messages or figuring out the issue independently before asking others for help.

**Asking or answering a smart-question:** I did not use AI very often for smart-questions themselves, but I did use it to better understand what actually makes a question “smart” and how to ask questions more effectively on places like Stack Overflow. AI helped me understand the importance of including details such as error messages, expected behavior, and what I had already tried before asking for help.

**Coding example:** I frequently asked AI for coding examples, such as implementing TypeScript classes or writing code that would wrap text around an image. Usually, I would ask AI to generate an example or structure first, then I would adjust the image path or styling myself afterward. The examples rarely worked perfectly right away, but they usually gave me enough structure to understand how I should approach the assignment.

**Explaining code:** I often pasted code into ChatGPT and asked it to explain errors or unexpected output. During the final project, I sometimes used AI to better understand code written by other team members before modifying anything myself. For example, I used it to help explain how data was being passed between components, which users had access to certain pages, and how different TSX components were connected together throughout the project. This made it easier for me to understand how I should implement or call on components that I created myself.

**Writing code:** AI was heavily used throughout the semester for writing and debugging code. I often provided my existing code or whatever I had already completed and asked AI to help refine it or continue building from there. If I did not know where to even start approaching a problem, I would sometimes ask AI to generate scaffolding or starter structures that I could build off of myself. Although it saved a lot of time, there were moments where I relied on it too heavily instead of fully thinking through the problem on my own.

**Documenting code:** I did not use AI very heavily for documenting code itself, since most of the class did not require large amounts of documentation or comments. However, I occasionally used AI to help organize comments, improve readability, and generate cleaner commit or merge descriptions during the final project so the repository history was easier to follow.

**Quality assurance:** AI was very useful for debugging and quality assurance tasks. I often asked it to help clean up indentation, organize sections of code, or explain why certain layouts or components were behaving incorrectly. One common issue I dealt with was columns or text not aligning properly within layouts, so I would ask AI to help identify what was causing the formatting problem. Sometimes fixing one issue with AI accidentally created another problem somewhere else in the project, which taught me to carefully review every suggested change before applying it.

**Other uses in ICS 314 not listed:** I also used AI to help refine my professional portfolio website and generate more professional sounding descriptions for projects. It also helped me identify technical skills that were actually relevant to include instead of overcrowding the page with unnecessary information. I also occasionally used AI to better understand how to navigate through GitHub itself whenever I was unsure where to find certain features, commits, branches, or project information.

# III. Impact on Learning and Understanding

AI had a major impact on my learning experience in ICS 314. It made debugging less frustrating, helped clarify concepts I did not fully understand, and allowed me to approach assignments with more confidence when I felt stuck. However, there were also times when I became too dependent on AI instead of fully struggling through problems on my own. Over time, I realized AI was most useful when I used it to support my learning instead of replacing it entirely. By the end of the semester, I became more comfortable identifying when AI-generated solutions actually made sense versus when they were unnecessarily complicated.

# IV. Practical Applications

Outside of ICS 314, I also used AI during my cybersecurity internship. I used it to troubleshoot client device issues when documentation was unclear and to help identify possible solutions by describing device symptoms or problems. I also used AI to rewrite CIS benchmark recommendations into more user-friendly guides for companies that were less technically experienced.

# V. Challenges and Opportunities

One challenge with AI is that it is not always accurate. Sometimes it generated overly complicated solutions, referenced the wrong files, or provided answers outside the intended scope of the course. Despite this, AI provides many opportunities in software engineering education because it can act as a tutor, debugger, and productivity tool when used responsibly.

# VI. Comparative Analysis

Compared to traditional teaching methods, AI-enhanced learning provides much faster feedback and more personalized assistance. Instead of waiting for office hours or discussion responses, I could quickly ask questions and experiment with different approaches without worrying about asking a “bad” question. At the same time, traditional teaching methods are still important because they help students develop the foundational problem-solving skills and critical thinking that AI alone cannot replace.

# VII. Future Considerations

I believe future software engineers will rely heavily on AI because it saves significant amounts of time, especially for repetitive or low-effort tasks. AI allows engineers to focus more on deeper problem solving and critical thinking rather than repetitive debugging or setup work. At the same time, students should still learn programming fundamentals independently so they can properly understand and verify AI-generated solutions.

# VIII. Conclusion

By the end of ICS 314, AI had become something I regularly relied on both for learning concepts and handling difficult assignments. Tools like ChatGPT and Claude helped me debug code, organize essays, understand unfamiliar code, and contribute more confidently during the final project. Although AI is not perfect and can sometimes encourage overreliance, I believe it is an extremely valuable tool in modern software engineering education when used responsibly.
